The basics

What is flexible dieting?

A way of eating that fits around your life, not the other way round. No banned foods, no cutting out food groups, no rigid meal plans. You hit a daily target for protein, carbs and fat that's right for your body and goal, and what you eat to get there is up to you.

Biscuit with your afternoon tea? Fine. Pizza on a Friday? That works too. It's your overall intake over time that decides your results, not any single food. Nobody has ever gained a stone from one slice of birthday cake, and nobody has ever got lean from one salad either.

Why most diets fail (and it isn't willpower): they're built on rules you'd never keep for a year. Then a birthday, a holiday or a bad week happens and the whole thing collapses. Flexible dieting has nothing to fall off, because there are no rules to break. A takeaway isn't a failure. It's just food you account for.

What it isn't: "eat whatever you want and hope for the best". You still have targets, and you still have to be honest. The flexibility is in which foods you choose, not in whether the numbers matter.

The golden rule: swap like for like

  • Protein → protein (easy): chicken, tuna, eggs, Greek yoghurt, tofu, tempeh, Quorn, cottage cheese, whey. Match the protein grams, and watch the fat (salmon has far more than chicken).
  • Carbs → carbs (easy): rice, sweet potato, oats, sourdough, pasta, potato, fruit. Weigh both uncooked: 80g dry rice is close to 80g dry pasta.
  • Fats → fats (check the numbers): oils, avocado, nuts, nut butters, egg yolks. At 9 kcal/g, small differences matter, so weigh them.
Step 3

Tracking your food

Flexible dieting only works if you know what you're actually eating. Small inaccuracies add up to several hundred calories a day, and it's the most common reason people "do everything right" but don't see results. MyFitnessPal, Cronometer and MacroFactor all work. The app matters less than how you use it.

Step 4

Build your day: protein first

Protein first. Every meal. Every time. It's the hardest macro to hit if you leave it until the end of the day. Nobody has ever happily eaten 60g of protein at 10pm.
  1. Decide how many meals you're eatingYour protein gets divided across them
  2. Pick your protein source firstWeigh it raw, log it
  3. Split your protein roughly evenlyThe calculator above does this for you
  4. Add carbs around itWeigh uncooked. More around training days
  5. Fats fill in lastCheck what's already come in with your protein
  6. Check your totalsWithin 100–150 kcal and close on protein is absolutely fine

Carbs & fats worth knowing

  • Carbs: rice, pasta, oats, potato and sourdough are the most calorie-efficient way to hit your target. Weigh them dry. Sugar and honey count too: two sugars in five brews a day is 160 kcal you haven't accounted for.
  • Fats: a tablespoon of olive oil is ~120 kcal. The free-pour glug is roughly three tablespoons, and it is undefeated at ruining otherwise excellent weeks. Bake or air fry to save 100–200 kcal a meal.
  • Fibre: aim for 25–35g a day from oats, veg, legumes, fruit and wholegrains. It keeps you fuller and stops the 10pm cupboard raid.
  • Free flavour: herbs, spices, vinegar, lemon, mustard and hot sauce are effectively calorie-free. Dieting does not require sad, beige chicken.

My rule for eating out: I budget 1,000–1,500 kcal for the meal before I leave the house, then build the day around it. Restaurants use far more oil and butter than you would. Better still, check the menu online first and pre-log it.

Yes, you can have the cake. If it fits your targets, it fits your plan. Swap it in rather than add it on. Just be honest about how often. "It fit my macros" stops being a defence somewhere around the fourth consecutive day of it.

Step 8

There's much more to a great physique

Nutrition is one piece of the puzzle. No PDF has ever built anyone a set of shoulders. For real, lasting change you also need:

  • A training programme built around your goals, with progressive overload
  • Daily steps (NEAT), which can vary by 1,000+ kcal a day between people. An hour in the gym doesn't undo fourteen hours in a chair.
  • Cardio where it helps your goal
  • Structured phases: fat loss, maintenance and growth
  • Knowing when to adjust. Cutting another 300 calories because you're frustrated is not a strategy, it's a tantrum with a spreadsheet.
  • Accountability on the hard weeks, not just the easy ones

The 5 golden rules

  1. Protein firstEvery meal, every day
  2. Swap like for likeAnd weigh everything uncooked
  3. Manage your day, not just your mealsPlan ahead, not after
  4. Track consistently, not perfectlyNobody hands out medals for a flawless fortnight followed by three months off
  5. Consistency beats perfectionClose enough, most of the time, for long enough
