What is a 1RM?

Your one-rep max (1RM) is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. It's a handy reference point for planning heavy training, but you don't need to actually test it to know roughly what it is.

This calculator estimates it from a set you've already done, using a standard formula (weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)).1 Because most people don't train to failure, it adds the reps you had left in the tank, so the estimate reflects what you could really have done.2

Only lifts that are sensible to max are in the list: big, stable barbell and machine lifts. Single-leg work, isolation exercises and anything wobbly aren't, because they're not safe or useful to test at one rep.

Most accurate from sets of 2–10 reps taken close to failure. Above about 10 reps the estimate gets wobbly, so use a heavier set if you can.
02

How to use the numbers

  • Strength sets (1–5 reps, like 3×3 or 3×5) usually sit around 80–90% of your 1RM at 0–3 reps in reserve. Only go to failure when it's safe (safety bars, a spotter or a machine), and never on deadlifts
  • Hypertrophy sets (6–30 reps) don't need percentages at all. Warm up at about RPE 6, then do 2 working sets in your rep range, as close to failure as possible (0–3 reps left)
  • Track it over time. Work it out from your best heavy set every few weeks. If the number's going up, you're getting stronger, no max test needed
03

Should you actually test a 1RM?

For most people, most of the time: no. An estimate tells you almost as much with a fraction of the risk and fatigue. "I just wanted to see" is how most gym injuries start.

A real max test makes sense when

  • You've been lifting consistently for at least 6 months and your technique on that lift is solid and repeatable
  • It's planned at the end of a strength block, after a lighter few days, not dropped into a random Tuesday
  • You're well slept, well fed and healthy, ideally at maintenance or in a surplus, not deep in a fat loss phase
  • You've got safety bars or a spotter

Don't test a max if

  • You're a beginner. You're still learning the lift, and your strength is going up every week anyway
  • You're injured, ill, run down or in a hard fat loss phase
  • It's on a lift that isn't safe to max: single-leg work, isolation exercises, anything wobbly
  • You tested one less than about 8–12 weeks ago
